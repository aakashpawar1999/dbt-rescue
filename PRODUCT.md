# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Citizens such as Sunita Devi or Meena Kumari who are checking a government
benefit payment on a phone, often with limited digital or English fluency. A
family member or service-centre operator may assist them.

## Product Purpose

DBT Rescue turns a fragmented payment status into one understandable recovery
journey: where the fictional payment stopped, what that means, who can fix it,
what to carry, and how to follow the next state. Success is one citizen
completing the recovery path without entering sensitive information or being
sent to the wrong organisation.

## Brand Personality

Clear, calm, practical. The interface should lower anxiety, make one next step
obvious, and earn trust through precise limits rather than decoration.

## Anti-references

- Technical government-status dashboards that expose codes without an action.
- Chatbots that invent banking remedies or hide uncertainty behind confidence.
- Official-looking branding that implies government ownership or endorsement.
- Feature-heavy fintech shells that bury the user's immediate recovery task.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with where, why, and what next.
2. Explain in plain language first; keep technical evidence available but secondary.
3. Make the responsible organisation and carry-list unmistakable.
4. Show source, timestamp, and simulation status instead of implying certainty.
5. Keep the journey usable on a narrow phone, by keyboard, and with assistive technology.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA basics for the prototype: semantic landmarks and headings,
visible keyboard focus, text labels, live status announcements, contrast that
does not rely on colour alone, a 360 px mobile baseline, readable English copy,
and no sensitive input fields. Keep the main action and the current state
visible without requiring a gesture, hover, or audio.
